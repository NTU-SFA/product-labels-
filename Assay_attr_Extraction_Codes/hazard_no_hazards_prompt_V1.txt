CRITICAL — distinguish the PRODUCT from the HAZARD:
A subject usually names a product (what is sold) AND a hazard (what is wrong with it).
You must label only the HAZARD, never the product.
- "Aflatoxins in almonds from the US" -> the product is almond, the hazard is Aflatoxins -> ["Aflatoxins"].
- "Lead in turmeric" -> product turmeric, hazard Lead -> ["Lead"].
- A food name (almond, milk, peanut, soy, sesame, hazelnut, etc.) is usually the PRODUCT,

- The product and the hazard can occasionally be the SAME attribute (e.g. an unauthorised
  novel-food substance that is itself the marketed item). In that case the hazard label still
  describes the problem, not the product identity.

ALLERGENS — special care:
Allergens are usually ordinary foods (almond, milk, egg, soy, peanut, gluten, mustard, etc.).
- If such a food is mentioned as an UNDECLARED / not-declared / not-mentioned-on-label /
  mislabelled / "contains traces of" allergen, the hazard is the GENERIC label "Allergens"
  (NOT the food name, which is not even an allowed hazard label).
  e.g. "Undeclared almond in cookies" -> ["Allergens"];
       "Milk not mentioned on the label of dark chocolate" -> ["Allergens"].
- If that same food is simply the product and the hazard is something else, label the other hazard.
  e.g. "Salmonella in almonds" -> ["Salmonella spp"] (almond is the product).

Common no-hazard subject patterns -> hazard_label:
1. Foreign matter (glass, plastic, metal, wood, stones, nails, insects/dead insects, fragments,
   particles) -> "Foreign bodies".
2. Missing/absent/invalid official or health certificate, CHED, documentation issues -> "Documentation".
3. Labelling deficiencies / incorrect / missing labelling (not allergen-specific) -> "Labelling".
4. Unauthorised novel food / novel food ingredient (unapproved plant, extract, NMN, CBD, etc.)
   -> "Novel food" (or the specific allowed substance label if one applies).
5. Unauthorised substance / unauthorised or unapproved plant species / unapproved ingredient
   with no more specific allowed label -> "Unauthorised substance".
6. Unauthorised food additive(s), or a named additive / E-number -> the specific allowed additive
   label if listed (e.g. "E171 Titanium dioxide"), otherwise "Food additives".
7. Poor temperature control / broken cold chain -> "Temperature control".
8. Abnormal smell / odour, organoleptic defects or changes -> "Organoleptic aspects".
9. Mould / mold / moulds -> "Mould".
10. Illegal / unauthorised import, smuggling, no import authorisation -> "Illegal import".
11. Veterinary checks/control issues -> "Veterinary control"; feed additive issues -> "Feed additives".
12. A named pathogen (Salmonella, Listeria, E. coli, Norovirus, Hepatitis A, etc.) -> the matching
    allowed pathogen label; a specific Salmonella serotype -> its allowed label if present, else "Salmonella spp".
13. A named pesticide active substance (chlorpyrifos, prochloraz, thiacloprid, ...) -> its allowed
    label if present, else "Pesticide residues". Ethylene oxide -> "Ethylene oxide".
14. Prefer the most specific allowed label the subject supports; otherwise use the broad allowed label.
